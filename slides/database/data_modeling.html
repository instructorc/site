Learning Activity: Neighborhood Watch

A neighborhood wants to launch a watch program and has hired your team to design its database. They need to track volunteers, incidents, meeting agendas, and an evening watch schedule.

Their website will show three pages:

  1. A watch list with each volunteer's name, phone, and email
  2. A list of all incidents
  3. Links to upcoming and past meeting agendas

Your goal: turn this request into the first draft of an ERD in four rounds.

Teams of 3 or 4  |  Roles: Interviewer, Recorder, Organizer, Presenter  |  About 35 minutes

Form teams and assign roles before showing round 1. Each team needs sticky notes in three colors (or a shared FigJam, Miro, or draw.io board for online sections).

Round 1: Interview the Client (8 min)

I am the president of the neighborhood association. Write your questions first, then you get to ask me 3.

WHO | WHAT | WHEN | RULES | PRIVACY

Write down every answer. Each one is a design decision.

CLIENT ANSWER CARD (give the same answer to any team that asks): Volunteers must be residents of the neighborhood; a few local business owners also volunteer. Only volunteers can log an incident; residents call or email a volunteer. Incident types: suspicious activity, theft, vandalism, noise, other. One incident can have more than one type. We need date, time, location (nearest address or intersection), description, and who logged it. Watch runs 6 PM to midnight, every evening, in two shifts (6 to 9 and 9 to midnight). Each shift needs 2 volunteers. Volunteers can sign up for as many shifts as they want. Volunteers choose whether their phone number shows on the public site. Email always shows. Agendas are PDFs in our Google Drive. We just need the meeting date, a title, and the link. Meetings are monthly at the library. Only the site administrator (also a volunteer) posts agendas. Keep incidents for two years. We would love to know which blocks have the most incidents. If asked something not listed: "Good question, we have not decided. What would you recommend?"

Round 2: Data Dump (7 min)

Write one piece of data per sticky note; teams can use one text box per item in draw.io. Aim for at least 25.

  • Start from the website. Look at each of the three pages and list every value it shows.
  • Then use the interview. Every answer you got should produce at least one note.
  • Be specific. Write first name and last name, not just name.

No grouping yet. Quantity first.

Round 3: Group and Cut (10 min)

  1. Cluster notes that describe the same thing.
  2. Name each cluster with a singular noun in caps: VOLUNTEER, not Volunteers. These are your candidate entities. The notes inside are attributes.
  3. Sort every note:
    • ■ Keep: the site or the client needs it
    • ■ Cut: nice to know, but nobody asked for it
    • ■ Insight: helps the community make decisions
  4. Mark the one attribute in each entity that makes every row unique.

Minimum data rule: all that is needed is there, and all that is there is needed.

Round 4: Connect and Defend (8 min)

Draw a line between entities that relate. For each line, write one sentence:

A [ENTITY] can [verb] one or more [ENTITY].

Example: A VOLUNTEER can log one or more INCIDENTS.

Expected entities: VOLUNTEER, INCIDENT, INCIDENT_TYPE, SHIFT, MEETING (agenda link can live here or in AGENDA). Watch for teams that put "volunteer 1, volunteer 2" columns on SHIFT, or a list of types on INCIDENT. Those are the hooks for the bridge entity discussion later (same idea as PROP_AMEN_LIST in the MPLS Renters example). Common cuts: home address, birth date, emergency contact (unless the client asked). Insight examples: incident location plus date (which blocks, which nights), shift fill rate. The relationship sentences lead straight into the Business Rules slides.

Debrief

  1. Which client answer changed your design the most?
  2. Where did two teams disagree? Who was right, or does it depend?
  3. Which group was hardest to name, and what does that tell you?

Exit ticket: List your final entities, the unique attribute for each, and your two best relationship sentences.

Keep your board. We will turn it into business rules and a conceptual model next.

Data Modeling

Data Models + Data Modeling

Model: Abstraction of a real-world object or event

Data Models

  • Are a communication tool
  • Give an overview of the database
  • Organize data for various users
  • Are an abstraction for the creation of good database

Data Modeling

  • Iterative and progressive process of creating a specific data model for a determined problem domain
  • The process of transforming logical data models into physical data Models

Business Rules

Definition

Brief, precise, and unambiguous description of a policy, procedure, or principle.
Some business rule describes a constraint that cannot be shown in the ER diagram

Purpose

Describe main and distinguishing characteristics of the data

Example

- A community watch group member can submit one or more incidents.
- An incident can be assigned multiple categories
- An agenda can be only be created by site administrator who is also a member

Data Modeling

Conceptual Design

  • Design a database independent of database software and physical details Conceptual data model – Describes main data entities, attributes, relationships, and contraints

Logical Design

  • The process of transforming (or mapping) a conceptual schema of the application domain into a schema for the data model
  • some software is typically to create and map this model

Physical Design

  • modifies how the data is formatted (framed) and transported between the client and server, both of which manage the entire process, and hides all the complexity from our applications within the new framing layer
  • The process of transforming logical data models into physical data Models

Understanding various keys

Types of Keys:

  • Primary Key is a column or group of columns in a table that uniquely identify every row in that table
  • Foriegn Key is a column that creates a relationship between two tables
  • Composite/Compound is a combination of two or more columns that uniquely identify rows in a table
  • Alternate Key iis a column or group of columns in a table that uniquely identify every row in that tables

How are keys applied?

  • In a relationship, the many side recieves the foriegn key
  • There are so many keys - The difference sometimes equates to what can uniquely identify tuples(a column) or rows
